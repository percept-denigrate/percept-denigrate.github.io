Oxalate is an anion with formula $C_2O_4^{2-}$, linked to oxalic acid $H_2C_2O_4$. Compounds containing oxalate or oxalic acid are found in some plant foods, especially spinach, soy, sweet potato, or kale.

A study showed a link between oxalates consumption and cancer.[^1] They injected mice with three substances: oxaloacetate, saline and acetic acid.

The survival rates of mice are as follows:

![[oxalates_mouse_survival.png]]

The red arrows represent injection, the purple arrow represents apparition of the first tumor, and the blue arrow represents the time when all mice had tumors. The green line is the population of mice injected with oxalate, the blue and red line is the control group.

All mice injected with oxalates had cancer by day 75, and were dead by day 90. The two control groups lived over 200 days, well over the life expectancy for mice. That's sus.

They also measured the amount of oxalates in tumors.

![[oxalates_tumor.png]]

But this pattern also appears in huma subjects, who we don't know have been exposed to more oxalates. It's entirely possible oxalates are produced by or accumulate in cancer cells.

A mechanism has been proposed. The c-Fos protein associates with the c-Jun protein into the AP-1 complex. AP-1 then binds the genes for cellular divison. An overactivation of this mechanism can cause cancer. c-Fos is highly present when cells are exposed to oxalates.

![[oxalates_cfos.png]]

The green shows c-Fos, the blue shows DNA.

# But

A similar experiment has been made, but by injecting the solutions in mice's back.

![[oxalates_mouse_survival_back.png]]

Mice who received the oxalates injection in their back didn't die.

This means oxalates only negatively affect some tissue.

They also wrote that they did a similar experiment on mice from another  species, but the mice didn't get cancer. The mouse species where the effect was observed don't have a thymus.

[^1]: [Castellaro et al. (2015), *Oxalate induces breast cancer*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26493452/)
