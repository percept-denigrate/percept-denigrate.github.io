The Big five is a model for personality. It consists of 5 scores representing different aspects of personality:
- Extroversion: tendency to be outgoing and to seek high-stimulation environments
- Neuroticism: tendency for stress and negative feelings
- Agreeableness: tendency to act kindly and avoid conflict
- Conscientiousness: discipline, capacity to plan and organize tasks
- Openness: tendency to seek new experiences

This model is preferred over many others models such as MBTI, as it is relatively stable throughout time[^1] and predictive of life outcomes. In particular, conscientiousness is predictive of academic success[^2] and job performance[^3], even after controlling for [[IQ]]. Agreeableness and low neuroticism are more moderately predictive of these outcomes, but are also predictive of relationship satisfaction[^4].

[^1]: [Atherton et al. (2023), *Stability and Change in the Big Five Personality Traits: Findings from a Longitudinal Study of Mexican-Origin Adults*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8821110)

[^2]: [Zell & Lesick (2022), *Big five personality traits and performance: A quantitative synthesis of 50+ meta-analyses*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34687041/)

[^3]: [van Aarde et al. (2017), *The validity of the Big Five personality traits for job performance: Meta-analyses of South African studies*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34687041/)

[^4]: [Malouff et al. (2012), *The Five-Factor Model of personality and relationship satisfaction of intimate partners: A meta-analysis*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0092656609002001)
